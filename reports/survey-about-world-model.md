# World Models: From Latent Imagination to Interactive Environments

## TL;DR

- World Models demonstrates that compressed representations of an environment can support a compact policy, including training inside a learned simulated environment and transferring the policy back to the original environment.[1]
- Dreamer makes latent imagination a mechanism for learning behavior: it propagates gradients through imagined trajectories rather than requiring every policy-learning step to interact with the real environment.[2]
- DreamerV3 emphasizes breadth and robustness, reporting results across more than 150 tasks with one configuration and collecting diamonds in Minecraft without human data or curricula.[3]
- Genie learns an interactive environment and latent action interface from unlabeled videos. Its ability to generate controllable frames addresses a different objective from Dreamer's reward-directed behavior learning.[4]
- Long-context video-language modeling adds another dimension: the Large World Model paper describes 7B models processing sequences exceeding one million tokens. Context capacity alone, however, is not evidence of reliable action-conditioned simulation.[5]

## Background

For this report, a world model is a learned representation of an environment's temporal evolution that can support prediction, imagined interaction, or learning behavior. This working definition groups together related but distinct objectives: compressed simulation in World Models, latent policy learning in Dreamer, and generated interactive environments in Genie.[1][2][4]

The distinction matters because the phrase does not identify a single benchmark or architecture. A model can be useful for controlling an existing task without producing visually elaborate videos; conversely, generating an interactive visual environment is not the same result as learning a successful policy in an external environment. Dreamer and Genie illustrate these different evaluation targets.[2][4]

The historical progression represented here is from compact learned simulations in 2018, to latent-imagination control in 2019, to broader control robustness in DreamerV3 and large-scale video-based models in 2023–2024.[1][2][3][4][5]

## Theme 1: Compressed Simulation and Latent Imagination

World Models learns compressed spatial and temporal representations without requiring supervised labels for that representation-learning stage. It then uses the learned features as inputs to a compact policy. The paper reports that a policy trained within the model's generated “dream” can transfer to the actual environment.[1]

Dreamer shares the motivation of learning through an internal model but changes how behavior is optimized. It learns behavior by propagating analytic gradients of learned state values through trajectories imagined in a compact latent state space. The authors report comparisons on 20 visual-control tasks, with advantages in data efficiency, computation time, and final performance over the approaches considered in the paper.[2]

The useful comparison is therefore not simply whether both systems predict future states. World Models demonstrates the practicality of a compressed learned simulation and a compact controller; Dreamer makes differentiable latent imagination central to behavior learning. Both exploit learned internal environments, but their reported contributions concern different parts of the learning procedure.[1][2]

## Theme 2: Robustness Across Control Tasks

DreamerV3 explicitly targets a limitation in general reinforcement learning: applying an algorithm to a new domain can require substantial configuration and experimentation. Its authors report outperforming specialized methods across more than 150 tasks while using a single configuration.[3]

The paper attributes stable cross-domain learning to techniques involving normalization, balancing, and transformations. It also reports collecting diamonds in Minecraft from scratch, without human data or curricula, as an example of learning in an open-world setting with sparse rewards.[3]

Compared with the earlier Dreamer results on 20 visual-control tasks, DreamerV3 broadens the reported task coverage and emphasizes reducing domain-specific tuning. These task counts should not be interpreted as a direct performance ratio: the two abstracts describe different evaluation sets, and do not establish a common numerical scale.[2][3]

The evidence supports a narrower conclusion than “a general model of the physical world”: DreamerV3 reports broad competence in its tested control domains. Establishing transfer to arbitrary environments would require additional evidence beyond the reported task collection.[3]

## Theme 3: Learning Interactive Environments from Video

Genie shifts attention from an agent learning a reward-directed policy to a model generating an environment that users can act within. The paper describes an 11B-parameter system consisting of a spatiotemporal video tokenizer, an autoregressive dynamics model, and a latent action model.[4]

Its distinctive data condition is the absence of ground-truth action labels. Genie learns a latent action interface from unlabeled Internet videos and uses it for frame-by-frame interaction. The paper also discusses using that learned action space to help agents imitate behaviors from previously unseen videos.[4]

The retrieved introduction distinguishes the initial corpus of more than 200,000 hours of public Internet gaming videos from the filtered set of 30,000 hours used to train the final model on hundreds of 2D platformer games. Those quantities describe different stages of data selection, not interchangeable measures of final training data.[4]

Genie and Dreamer consequently should not be ranked by model size or apparent visual richness alone. Genie demonstrates learned interaction without action annotations; Dreamer reports behavior-learning performance on control tasks. A useful comparison must specify whether the target is generated-environment interaction, policy quality, or transfer to an external task.[2][4]

## Theme 4: Long Context Is a Different Capability

World Model on Million-Length Video And Language With Blockwise RingAttention focuses on long-context sequence modeling. It describes progressive extension from 4K to 1M tokens, long-context data curation, an open-source scalable training implementation, and a family of 7B-parameter models for long text and video sequences.[5]

This work is relevant to temporal coverage: processing long videos and documents potentially provides access to information spanning much longer histories. Its reported contributions concern language retrieval and long-video understanding.[5]

That is different from the action-conditioned interaction described by Genie and the imagined behavior optimization described by Dreamer. A long context window is a capacity statement; it is not, by itself, a demonstration that a model can predict the consequences of arbitrary interventions or train a successful controller.[2][4][5]

A practical taxonomy should therefore distinguish at least three questions: how far a model can observe into a sequence, whether it supports controllable transitions, and whether imagined transitions improve behavior learning. The cited systems supply evidence for different parts of that taxonomy rather than a single common capability score.[2][4][5]

## Trends and open problems

### Trends supported by this source selection

The selected work shows two complementary directions: greater robustness across control tasks in DreamerV3, and larger-scale learning from video in Genie and the long-context World Model paper. These are trends within this 2018–2024 selection; this report does not claim they exhaust the state of research in 2026.[3][4][5]

A second direction is changing what supervision is required. Genie demonstrates learning an interactive latent action interface without ground-truth action labels, while the earlier control papers emphasize using learned models to support policy learning.[1][2][4]

### Open questions motivated by the comparison

The following are synthesis questions raised by the different reported objectives, not claims that a cited paper has already solved them:

- **Evaluation alignment:** How should interactive-environment quality, long-video understanding, and reward-directed control be compared without treating them as equivalent achievements? The cited papers evaluate different capabilities.[2][4][5]
- **Transfer:** Under what conditions do behaviors learned inside a model remain useful outside it? World Models reports a transfer example, but that result does not establish universal transfer guarantees.[1]
- **Action meaning:** How can latent actions learned from video be aligned with the available actions of an external agent? Genie's learned latent action space makes this a concrete interface question.[4]
- **Robustness versus universality:** How much does success across a large tested task set predict performance in an unseen domain? DreamerV3 provides broad reported coverage, but the abstract does not establish unrestricted generalization.[3]
- **Context versus dynamics:** When does access to a long history improve controllable future prediction rather than only retrieval or understanding? Long-context modeling and interactive generation offer distinct evidence that should be assessed separately.[4][5]

Overall, the strongest conclusion is that world-model research spans multiple useful capabilities. The selected papers demonstrate compressed simulation, latent behavior learning, cross-task robustness, learned interaction from unlabeled video, and long-context video-language processing. A responsible survey preserves those distinctions instead of treating every model called a “world model” as a validated universal simulator.[1][2][3][4][5]
